Portugal starts in 2000 with a socialist government, membership of the European Union, the
Eurozone and NATO, five divisions of which two garrison islands, and a debt eight times the
size of its treasury. It also starts with more influence in six former colonies than any
other power holds there, and no instrument between a diplomatic note and a war.

The tree is about building that instrument.

## Political Situation

The Socialist Party governs with 45.2% support. The Social Democrats are the alternative
government at 33.1%, the CDU holds 9.2% on the left and the CDS-PP 8.5% on the right. The
Braganças and the national right are rounding errors that some branches of the tree do not
leave as rounding errors.

## The Tree

Seven columns, each with its own button at the top of the tree: Ideology (the Ideological
Question), Economy (the Programme of Government over Regional Development), The Navy (the
Portuguese Sea), The Army (the Armed Forces), The Air Force, Lusophone World (the Lusophone
Community over the Campaigns) and The Powers (the Balance of Powers over the Dream of Iberia).
The European Union's shared trees sit below the first two columns.

No focus waits for money in the treasury: whatever a focus costs beyond what Portugal holds is
borrowed. Focus bonuses collect in three national spirits: The Portuguese Economy, The Third Republic and
The Armed Forces. Separate spirits are kept only for the shadow war (The Lusophone Network, The
Weight of Distance, Overseas Doctrine and Under Investigation) and the Anglo-Portuguese Alliance.

- **Lusophone Community**: the Lusophone Community, the Camões Institute and economic
  diplomacy, the civilian and officer networks and the Legion, the intelligence service, the
  European question and the Fifth Empire.
- **Ideological Question**: an ideological development meter that rises on every change of
  government and every policy enacted, and gates the radical content behind it. The current value
  and tier show in the description of The Third Republic. The party press, party machine and
  political university each add daily support for the ruling outlook, and the Beijing,
  Washington and sovereigntist alignments add more. Four branches:
  - **The Battle of Ideas**: the party press and the institutes, then the Casino Conferences
    and a choice of tradition. Down one side are the Hidden King, Integralismo Lusitano,
    Pessoa's Mensagem and Lusotropicalism. Down the other are Seara Nova, Agostinho da Silva,
    Eduardo Lourenço and the critics of Lusotropicalism. Both meet at the Greatest Portuguese,
    where the country crowns Salazar, Cunhal or Aristides de Sousa Mendes.
  - **Public Opinion**: focuses that move popularity and put people in the street, then a
    general strike or a state of exception. Either one opens the Desperate Generation and
    Revolutionary Rupture, a revolution the AI never takes. The Desperate Generation leads on to
    the Troika protests, a presidential dissolution that calls an early election, and the new
    parties of the right.
  - **The Memory of April**: Grândola, the captains, the first May Day and the Constituent
    Assembly, then the Hot Summer or the 25th of November and the Constitution of 1976. The four
    founders follow: Cunhal, Soares, Sá Carneiro and Freitas do Amaral. The left's Contraption
    or the right's Democratic Alliance follows, depending on who won the last election.
  - **International Solidarity**: alignment focuses that open Beijing, Washington, the
    sovereigntist bloc, BRICS or the Lusophone world depending on who is in office, then Europe
    with Us or Proudly Alone.
- **The regime directions** close the four branches: the Crown follows the Greatest
  Portuguese, the Executive State the new parties of the right, the Revolution April, Always,
  and the Open Republic the Tenth of June. Each needs its own parties in government and
  ideological development above 60, and only one can be taken per game. Each has three core
  focuses, a choice of specialisations that only add modifiers, and an endgame.
  - **The Crown** (PPM, CDS-PP, NOS): a monarchy referendum that passes if the monarchists hold
    more than 20% or stability is above 60%. If another crown party governs, a win also hands the
    government to the monarchists. If they already govern, it only crowns the king. Then the
    Kingdom of Portugal, a crowned republic, an integral monarchy or the Sebastianist dream, and
    the Lusophone Crown, which makes Portugal the Fifth Portuguese Empire, or the Fifth Iberian
    Empire once the Iberian Union has formed.
  - **The Executive State** (PDR, PNR, the Army): the constitution suspended and elections
    ended, which Brussels answers with an Article 7 procedure. The Guardian Council, the Fourth
    Republic or the Chief Executive's shareholder state, and a permanent executive at the end.
  - **The Revolution** (CDU, POUS, PCTP, BE): the Alentejo's land reform, Lajes closed and NATO
    left, popular power, the parish republic or carnation socialism, and a Carnation Bloc of
    Lusophone comrades.
  - **The Open Republic** (PSD, PS, Nós, Cidadãos!, MPT): a federalist Lisbon, the European
    Vanguard or the Atlantic Hub, and a second Treaty of Lisbon.
- **Armed Forces**, **Portuguese Sea** and **Air Force**: professionalization,
  procurement, and a doctrine school in each service. Almost nothing is mutually exclusive;
  the time and the money make the choice. Each service can buy a research facility for
  15 billion early on: Santa Margarida and the Viana do Castelo yards open right after the
  service's first focus, and the Évora aeronautics park after the F-16 upgrade. Behind each
  facility are five research programs for 25 billion each that cut three years off the penalty
  for researching ahead of time. They branch rather than run in a line: the Army's facility
  opens armour, the battle network and artillery side by side, and each later program opens
  from either of two earlier ones, so a player can skip what they do not need. Three finished
  programs in a service open a second facility for 25 billion. A state holds only one research
  facility of any kind. Lisbon and the Algarve start with one, and Central Portugal, Northern
  Portugal and the Alentejo are kept for the service facilities, so the second facilities and the
  civilian ones in regional development compete for Madeira, the Azores and any state Portugal
  gains. None of them is needed; they are for a player with money to spare. A program finished
  before the older technologies in its line are researched spends its uses on those instead.

  Each service also has a late game, built to bring the player back to it between wars:
  - **Fielding focuses** open once the matching technology is researched: stealth frigates,
    ocean-going submarines, hypersonic missiles and an unmanned squadron for the Navy; the
    soldier of the future, the networked division, long-range fires, a robotic brigade and
    layered air defence for the Army; loyal wingmen, stand-off weapons, a sixth-generation
    fighter and Space Command for the Air Force. Each costs money and gives research for the
    next generation of that equipment, three years ahead of time. After 2040 the Navy and the
    Army can rewrite their doctrine around them.
  - **Force-size focuses** need forces actually in service: the Atlantic Fleet more than 19
    ships, the Expeditionary Corps more than 75,000 men in formed units, An Air Force of
    Hundreds more than 249 aircraft, and the Carrier Air Wing a carrier at sea.
  - **Overseas focuses** need a subject or territory in a region and train for it. A subject in
    sub-Saharan Africa opens training missions (better in the heat, and experience for those
    subjects), the Gulf of Guinea patrol and air bases in Africa. South America opens the jungle
    warfare school, the South Atlantic station and an air bridge. Asia opens the Far East
    station and air wing. Moroccan territory opens the Army of Africa, Spanish territory the
    Peninsular Army, and Gibraltar or the western Mediterranean islands the Mediterranean
    Squadron. The capstones, Thalassocracy, An Army on Four Continents and Global Reach,
    need most of a service's regions.
  - **Naval Support decisions** help a small fleet. Bartolomeu Dias Frigates now delivers the two
    Dutch Karel Doorman hulls. After 2006 the player can buy another second-hand frigate for 1.5
    billion once a year, join an alliance naval exercise for navy experience in peacetime, or ask
    allied navies for cover in a war: 120 days of better naval coordination, detection and
    amphibious invasion.
- **Programme of Government**: the policy tree: financial, industrial and social policy, each
  a set of two-sided forks. Each side is open while any of its parties is in power, alone or
  in the governing coalition (for example PS, BE or CDU for the progressive property tax, and
  PSD, CDS-PP or NC for broadening the income tax). Taking a side commits the country to it, and reversing it needs a
  party of the other side in office. The reversal undoes the focuses as well as the
  effects, so an optimal set of policies means flipping governments deliberately. The
  branch also carries the court of auditors, the coalition agreements, and most of the
  reforms that improve the four starting spirits; the rest are in regional development.
  Social policy also answers Portugal's unemployment, which the economy counts as the workers left
  over once every sector is staffed. Labour Agreements send workers abroad (3% fewer in the workforce) and bring in
  remittances worth 0.1% of GDP a week. The CPLP Mobility Visa regularises Lusophone workers for
  more income tax and more influence abroad. The Student Pipeline buys research speed and
  productivity growth from the Lusophone world's best students.
  The wealth tax raises productivity growth and the flat tax lowers it. Retail CBDC
  (40 billion) gives every citizen an account at the central bank: inside the euro it is the
  digital euro, with the escudo back it is Portugal's own. The Sovereign Wealth Fund puts
  100 billion into international investments. It trades 6% of tax revenue, 10% of political
  power gain and a little productivity growth for 3% more return on investment and a lower
  interest rate. It cannot be founded alongside the Debt Brake at the end of consolidation.
  State Reform adds eight more forks of the same kind: public contracts, special tax regimes,
  market structure, the digital state, harm taxes, the car, criminal procedure and public pay.
  Its constitutional revision needs the PS or the PSD in power, then shrinks the Assembly
  and opens a research bench and a foreign funding register. The first focus of each side
  names the side it excludes and lists the focuses it would undo.
- **Regional Development**: seventy-two projects in seven regional columns (Norte, Centro,
  Lisboa, Alentejo, Algarve, Madeira and the Azores), each building in its own region. The
  early ones are the programmes of the 2000s: Leixões, the Douro, Autoeuropa, Sines, Alqueva and
  the islands' air links. Each opens when a matching policy focus is done, and the cohesion
  funds are free while Portugal is in the Union. Later projects open by date, from the Tâmega
  dams and EllaLink to the Porto–Vigo line, the Third Tagus Crossing and the Sines AI campus.
  The economy grows over the decades, so the later a project is, the more it builds for the
  time it takes: the projects of the 2030s and 2040s take 35 days and build three to five
  buildings. Madeira Tecnopolo and the AIR Centre each add a Civilian R&D Facility.
  Infrastructure, network infrastructure and rail
  terminals also need the railway or internet technology for the next level; where it is
  missing, the focus gives research toward it instead, at no cost.
- **Campaigns**: one chain per former colony, built on what actually happened there, and one for
  Western Sahara. They are small steps to take between the economic focuses, so almost all of
  them take 21 days.
- **Balance of Powers**: the Campaigns for powers that can answer back: Spain, the United
  Kingdom, the United States, China, Brazil and, if it forms without Portugal, the European
  Federation, each with a friendly and a hostile road.

Policy and industry focuses also move the interest groups, and the big settlements move them a
long way: compulsory letting costs 10 landowner opinion, the wealth tax costs them 15, and the
2012 labour law swings 15 from the unions to business. Repealing a policy takes back what it
gained. What it cost is not refunded.
Chinese contractors and planners anger the unions and the landowners.

A democratic Portugal that has suspended elections is not held to the democratic rule that wars
can only be justified on threat countries. The rule returns when elections do.

## The Shadow War

The loop is: build an intelligence network in a former colony, infiltrate its government and
army, buy influence there, sponsor a rising, send volunteers to the side you created, and puppet
whoever wins. Everything runs on three things the game already tracks: intelligence network
strength (built by operatives), the civilian and army infiltration assets (from the infiltration
operations), and influence (from the Campaign focuses). Portugal can send volunteers at any level
of world tension. The shadow war runs on focuses and operations only. It has no decision category
of its own.

A country opens to the shadow war when Portugal takes the hostile focus against it: the one at the
end of its Campaign, the Contest for Luanda for Brazil and the Almaraz Complaint for Spain. Cape
Verde, São Tomé, Cabinda, Andorra and Western Sahara are not targets. Gibraltar is not either,
because it is a British state, not a country. A country that has become Portugal's subject loses
the operations that can no longer reach it.

Portugal starts a civil war only where one is plausible, and always through a focus. The Return
of RENAMO, which needs ideological development above 40, 30% influence in Mozambique and Portuguese
advisers there, arms RENAMO and starts a Mozambican civil war with Portugal's politics in the rebel
government. The Madrid Operation, after the Almaraz Complaint in the Iberian question, needs a
government that is not Western Outlook, ideological development above 60, more than 90 network strength
and more than 40% influence in Spain, and Spanish influence in Portugal below 30%. For 20 billion it
does the same in Spain. Either way, Portugal then chooses which side to recognise. A civil war in
Brazil opens Establish an Aid Landing Ground, and one in Mozambique opens Secure Cahora Bassa. Both
are focuses after Nationals at Risk, which needs a government that is not Western Outlook and
ideological development above 60.

Four decision categories hold the rest of it. The Overseas War School, opened by the African War School,
sends an officer on a 120-day tour of a war Portugal has a hand in. The Imperial Conference,
opened by its focus, asks each Lusophone subject to join a federation. The Spanish Spoils are
described below. Counter-Intelligence belongs to the other side: a country carrying the second
or third tier of the Portuguese Presence spirit can spend 90 days purging Portugal's network
there, but only while Portugal has completed Lusophone Realpolitik and holds more than 39 network strength
in it. Portugal is told which country rolled up the network.

Backing a side in a war that was already running, such as Angola's, pays out when that side wins:
20% influence there, better trade relations, an infiltrated
government, political power and the resource rights of one of its states. The winner also
remembers Portugal as its liberator, a +100 opinion bonus that fades faster than a grievance such as
a seized Galicia, so old suspicions return in time. A war Portugal started
itself pays more: the winner becomes a subject, and Portugal gains 20%
influence there, trade relations, the resource rights of one of its states, 100 political power,
stability and war support.

A country that has just come out of a civil war is weak. When the Protectorate focus completes,
every independent country open to the shadow war, in Portugal's network, at peace with it and under
more than 50% Portuguese influence becomes the target of a one-year war goal to invade it and keep
it as a puppet, if it is below 30% stability or just out of a civil war.

Backing the weaker side, or a side the world has outlawed (a rebel movement, an unrecognized
government or a sanctioned country), pays more influence and more reward. The European question
decides what Brussels hears: Remain in Europe stands the operations down for good, and Atlantic
Exit, which needs a government that is not Western Outlook and ideological development above 40,
leaves the Union and NATO. The Second Wave sends larger volunteer forces and justifies war goals
faster.

Overseas intervention costs money as well as logistics. The Portuguese Legion is a weekly expense
of 0.2 billion for each country Portuguese volunteers are in. On top of that, fuel consumption,
equipment attrition and supply consumption rise with the number of those countries, which falls
hardest on formations Portugal cannot replace.

**The Lusophone Community** builds the instruments: a foreign intelligence service, an
operations section, the civilian and officer networks, and the Special Actions Detachment.

**Neo-colonialism** buys the Mining Concession, the Defence Agreement and the Economic
Protectorate. The Defence Agreement takes military access only from Portugal's client
governments, the rebel governments its risings set up.

**The campaigns** are the list of places to use the instruments, eight chains. Each one opens with a
cooperation focus that gives influence and opinion, and every cooperation step is a trade. Every
friendly step needs Portugal to hold more than 5% influence in the country and an opinion of Portugal
that is not hostile; the treaties need more influence and an opinion above 19 or 24. Portugal gives
what it has: Lisbon Advisers (5% stability, 5% less corruption, 5% cheaper bureaucracy), army experience
from a training mission, credit, and in a treaty a guarantee of independence and a Portuguese Concession.
The country gives what it has: the resource rights of a state with natural resources (Angola, Mozambique,
East Timor, Equatorial Guinea and Cabinda), military access (Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique, Cape Verde,
São Tomé and Cabinda), and migrant labour, which adds 1% to Portugal's workforce (Guinea-Bissau, East
Timor, Cape Verde and São Tomé). Guinea-Bissau, Cape Verde, São Tomé, East Timor and Equatorial Guinea
are too small for a civil war, so their opening focus is their only step before the choice: it gives 18 to
20% influence and seconds the advisers. The others run through cooperation focuses (Angola, Mozambique
and Cabinda) that add influence, advisers, training and credit, and end in the same choice between a
friendly road and a hostile one.

The Portuguese Concession is the price of a treaty and is never imposed. The country builds 15% faster,
gains 5% productivity growth and 4% population tax income, and loses 2% of its workforce, and 10% of its
civilian factories and 5% of its military factories work for Portugal. Its workers stay at
home, so Portugal needs no population to use them. A client that wins a civil war, or is bought by a
hostile focus, becomes an associated state instead: it gains 3% stability, 10% faster building and 3%
productivity growth, and gives 25% of both kinds of factory.

The trade carries a risk, and that risk is the point. Every adviser idea cuts the country's defence
against foreign influence by 10%. Portuguese banks hold the credit and Portuguese officers trained its
army, and the hostile focuses need exactly that: Portuguese advisers in place, 30% influence for the
small states, and 6 billion. None of them grant infiltration; that is done by hand through the operations.
Angola starts in a civil war that usually ends within a few years, so its chain works with or without the
war. Portugal observes from Estoril, seconds advisers to both sides, helps carry out the settlement with a
reconstruction credit for each, and bids for the oil and diamond contracts. Guinea-Bissau is the coup state,
where every officer above major did his staff course in Lisbon and two tons of cocaine a week come ashore
in the Bijagós. Mozambique is the dam Portugal still owned in 2000, the training mission and the Rovuma
gas. East Timor only exists from 2002. Equatorial Guinea joins the club in 2014 and brings Severo Moto
with it. Cabinda is not a country yet, and its first ministers are trained by Portuguese advisers.
The Republic of Cabinda declares it once ideological development is above 40 and whoever holds the
enclave is at war or below 40% stability. Cape
Verde is the one where nobody gets shot, and São Tomé is one island at the point every West African tanker
has to pass.

The two roads are mutually exclusive. The friendly focus is a treaty, a patronage or a settlement. For
Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique, East Timor, Equatorial Guinea, Cape Verde, São Tomé, Cabinda and the two
sides of Angola it is an offer the other side can refuse. The offer states what each side gives and gets.
Every treaty except Angola's needs more than 15% influence in the country, Cape Verde's associated
state 30%, and the Malabo Accord with Obiang's government also costs 60 political power.
An AI weighs it on the merits: it is more likely to accept when unstable, in deficit or at war, and less
likely when it dislikes Portugal or is already under 10 or 30% Portuguese influence. A refusal
uncompletes the focus, so the other road opens, and that country will not hear the offer again for a
year. The Portuguese advisers stay either way. The hostile focus usually asks nobody. It costs a
clear opinion penalty with the target, and it needs the advisers the cooperation focus placed.
For Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, East Timor and Equatorial Guinea the hostile focus opens the
country to the shadow war's operations. For the small states it is also a coup: with ideological
development above 20, 30% influence, advisers in place and 6 billion, the country becomes an
associated state at once and pays what a
winning client pays, with no civil war and no operation. For Cape Verde, São Tomé and Cabinda the
coup is the whole payoff and they are not shadow war targets. Angola is the
exception. While its civil war lasts, the hostile road against one side is the friendly road to the other:
Support UNITA (against Angola) and Recognise Luanda (against UNITA) are offers the backed side can
refuse. Support UNITA needs ideological development above 20. The backed side receives arms, army
experience, war support and credit at once, and Portugal is
paid only if it wins. If it accepts, the focus opens the other side to the shadow war. Once the war is over, Recognise Luanda is a standing treaty that Luanda can refuse: it receives a
reconstruction credit, army experience and political power, and Portugal receives a share of the oil.
Support UNITA funds UNITA as an opposition party without asking anyone: it costs stability in
Luanda and opinion, and opens Angola to the shadow war. The other hostile roads are the Night of
the Generals, the Return of RENAMO, the Petitioners, Severo Moto's Hour, the Malongo Fields against
Cabinda, the Question of Praia and the Sixteenth of July.

**Western Sahara** gets a chain of its own, for a government of PSD, Nós, Cidadãos! or PS, or of
CDU, POUS, PCTP or Bloco de Esquerda. The Sahrawi Question recognises the Sahrawi Republic, which
pleases it, Algeria, Cuba and Venezuela and angers Morocco. The Ultimatum to Rabat, the Kosovo
precedent applied to Western Sahara, needs war support above 60% and Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism. It demands that Morocco hand the
territory to the Sahrawi Republic, and a refusal gives Portugal a war goal on it. Free Sahara then
hands over whatever Portugal took and guarantees the Republic.

**The powers** are the Campaigns for countries that can answer back. Every Portugal can take
them. Each power has a friendly and a hostile road. Its summits and treaties need influence
there first: more than 5% in the United Kingdom, the United States and China (10% for the Macau
Free Port), and more than 10% in Spain and Brazil. The Azores Summit also costs 100 political
power and Champion EU-Mercosur 60. Two of the powers are still open to the shadow war:
the hostile Contest for Luanda opens Brazil to it, and the Almaraz
Complaint opens Spain. Spain: the Iberian summits, the
electricity market and the Albufeira water convention, or de-Spanishing, the Almaraz Complaint
and Aljubarrota. De-Spanishing also opens The Flag of Ceuta, which asks Madrid for Ceuta
alone and leaves a claim and a war goal on the city if Spain refuses. It needs a government
that is not Western Outlook and war support above 50%. The Almaraz Complaint also plants
agents in the Spanish government,
and while they stay there the Olivenza Incident turns a staged border incident into a war
goal on all of Spain; Aljubarrota and the Olivenza Incident exclude each other. Either war needs
a government that is not Western Outlook, ideological development above 60, war support above
70% and Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism. Either road ends in a proposal of union
(see below). The United Kingdom: the Falklands and Gibraltar declarations, the Hereford
courses, the Royal Navy's pre-1985 ships and a carrier built at Rosyth, delivered two years
after London takes the order, or the memory of the
1890 Ultimatum. These need only a good opinion in London, not world tension, and the surplus
ships are paid for whatever the treasury holds: what it cannot cover is borrowed. Royal Navy Licences costs a flat 8 billion, paid to London for the British
shipbuilding experts it sends. It grants naval research bonuses and licenses for the newest
British frigates, mine-hunter and landing ship, plus the newest destroyer and patrol boat if
London has built a modern one. The United States: Lajes, the Azores summit, an F-16 upgrade and the Atlantic Centre, or
ending the base agreement and leaving NATO. For the F-16 upgrade Lisbon asks for one, two or four
squadrons of 24 surplus F-16s at 0.25 billion each, and Washington sells all of them, half of them
or none. China: the Crisis Privatisations and golden visas, or screening Chinese capital and the
Macanese passports; either road then opens the Sines terminal and Macau, and Shenzhen
supply chains (industry and production research), then Chinese contractors and Chinese
planners, which cost money and political power and buy large cuts in construction time and
infrastructure upkeep. Brazil: the Treaty of Porto Seguro, then the Statute of Equality, a
stake in the pre-salt fields, Embraer at Évora (which Brazil can refuse) and championing
EU-Mercosur, or the Contest for Luanda, rejecting the spelling agreement and the Oi veto, then the
Treaty of Rio. From there a monarchist government can take a puppet war goal on Brazil and a
nationalist, fascist or military government can demand Bahia and Pernambuco; democratic and communist
governments cannot declare war on a government of their own ideology. Once Portugal holds Bahia
and Pernambuco, the Sunrise Invasion takes a puppet war goal on the rest of Brazil.
The Macanese passports bring two years of arrivals from Macau: more migration, faster research
and faster productivity growth. A focus that deals with one country improves that country's
trade relations with Portugal; Gibraltar with Madrid, for example, gives +20 with Spain.

**Macau.** While Washington has China under a trade war or an embargo, Portugal can offer to
take Macau back as a free port that carries Chinese goods past the sanctions. China answers
the offer. If it accepts, Portugal pays 60 billion up front, booked as 60 billion of
international investment, and 1 billion a month in rent. Macau becomes a Portuguese puppet,
and China takes the Macau Free Port spirit, which makes Chinese resources 15% cheaper for
Portugal: each civilian factory Portugal spends on Chinese trade buys about one more unit of
every resource. Full Sovereignty, which needs a government that is not Western Outlook, annexes
Macau outright. That ends the rent and the trade
discount, and China keeps up to 60 billion of Portugal's investments. Two Macau Garrison
brigades are raised in the city, and the People's Liberation Army contests it in a border war
from the Pearl River Delta. If Portugal wins, Macau stays Portuguese; if it loses, the city
returns to China; if neither side breaks within 90 days, the fighting stops and Macau stays
Portuguese. However it ends, Beijing then answers: while Portugal holds the city, China can
impose sanctions, take a war goal on Macau or protest; if China has retaken it, China can
impose sanctions or welcome Macau home.

**The Gates of Iberia** follows the proclamation of the union directly. The branch, the Crown of
Aragon below it and the South Atlantic Islands are part of the shared Dream of Iberia tree, so
whichever of Portugal or Spain forms the union gets them. Gates of Iberia grants a 5% foreign influence spirit
that Mare Nostrum later upgrades. The union can ask the United Kingdom for
Gibraltar, which London is likely to grant if Portugal kept the oldest alliance, in return for a
British base and military access (this needs more than 5% influence in the United Kingdom). If
London refuses, the union can ask again later or start a border war from Andalucia for the Rock,
which gets it expelled from the European Union while both are members. It can also ask Morocco for Tanger-Tetouan-Al Hoceima, which Rabat will refuse,
giving the union a war goal (this needs a union that is not Western Outlook with war support above
70% and Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism); and ask Andorra to join, which needs more than 30% influence there. The war over the Moroccan
region ends like any other, through the Treaty of Tangier or a negotiated peace. The Tangier Zone
cores the region either way, and a union that already owns it bypasses the focus and still gets
the core. The union's formal name follows the government (the Iberian Union,
Socialist Union, Confederation, State or Emirate), and so does the proclamation focus. A union
formed from the United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil and the Algarves becomes the United Kingdom of
Iberia, Brazil and the Algarves, and one formed from the Fifth Portuguese Empire becomes the Fifth
Iberian Empire.

**The South Atlantic Islands** demands Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha from London.
It needs a nationalist government, war support above 70%, Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism, a navy three times the Royal Navy's,
twice the United Kingdom's GDP, no faction with the United Kingdom, and at least one of the islands
still British.
An AI United Kingdom always cedes the islands it holds. A refusal adds the usual claims and war
goal, and the United Kingdom takes Iberian Sanctions: 5% less tax income and construction speed,
2% stability and 5% war support. The sanctions also come with a -40 opinion of the union. They end
when the United Kingdom cedes the islands through a decision, loses them, or the union no longer
exists.

The European Federation, if it forms without Portugal: bilateral agreements and an associated
partnership, or an Atlantic identity and closing the exclusive economic zone. What Portugal
asks of London, Washington, Beijing and Madrid arrives as an event they answer, and a refusal
uncompletes the focus so it can be asked again later.

**The open hand** drops the pretense. Two countries have a province Portugal can take while
they fight a civil war, each a short focus after Nationals at Risk. Establish an Aid Landing Ground takes Espírito
Santo and expands its airfield, because Brazil is an ocean away and helping anyone there needs a
local base. Secure Cahora Bassa takes Tete and the dam Portugal built. In Spain, Galicia follows
the Madrid Operation: while Spain is at war with itself or Portugal's government sits in Madrid,
it takes Galicia from a Spanish side that holds it and is not at war with Portugal. If Portuguese
troops hold Galicia instead, the focus annexes it for good. When the war ends, Portugal decides
whether to hand a held province back only if the winner is a government its own
rising installed: it can return it for influence or keep it. Any other winner, including a side
Portugal only backed, demands it back, and keeping it then gives that country a claim and a war
goal. If the side that
owned the province is annexed, the claim passes to the winner. At the far end there is a file on
Madrid, and after 2012 the Aljubarrota focus writes down claims on Galicia, Ceuta and Spain's
island states with a war goal to match.

**The Spanish Spoils** open whenever Portugal is at war with Spain, or Spain is fighting a
civil war Portugal started or took a side in. Three decisions take Ceuta, the Canary
Islands and the Balearic Islands: whatever Portugal occupies, or whatever a Spanish side at war
with itself holds and is not fighting Portugal over.

## The Lusophone Community

Lusophone Community, the first focus of the Lusophone World column, founds the Lusophone
Community and opens its decision category. Every member gains a membership spirit with 5% trade
opinion, and Portugal and each member gain 25 trade opinion of each other. The community grows in
stages, each a proposal its members vote on:

- **Common market**: needs one other member. The spirit rises to 15% trade opinion and 5%
  faster infrastructure building.
- **Mobility agreement**: needs the common market and two other members. Easier visas and work
  permits add 2% population growth and 1% research speed. It does not trouble Brussels.
- **Union**: the Free Movement Treaty needs the mobility agreement and three other members.
  Members gain 25% trade opinion, 10% faster infrastructure building, 5% more population growth
  and 3% research speed.
- **Federation**: the Federal Constitution needs two other members, two years of free movement
  and a founder outside the European Union. Every member that votes for it is annexed, and its
  states become cores. Members that vote against stay in the community, and the founder can offer
  each of them accession later, once a year.

Members join by invitation. Portugal can invite any Portuguese-speaking country (Angola, UNITA,
Brazil, Cabinda, Cape Verde, Equatorial Guinea, Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique, São Tomé and East
Timor) for 50 political power, and ask again after 180 days if it refuses. The invitation needs
the country's opinion of Portugal above 49, more than 10% Portuguese influence there, and that it
is not another country's subject. An AI then accepts four times in five, and Portugal's own
subject always does.

The founder puts proposals to the members: the common market, the mobility agreement, free
movement, a development fund (once there is a common market, 10 billion from the founder, after
which investment projects in every member, the founder included, cost 15% less), a mutual
defence clause (a guarantee for every member, including later ones) and the federal
constitution. The founder votes for each, every member votes, and the result arrives after 14
days. A proposal passes when more vote for it than against. Members can leave whenever they
choose. A member at war with the founder leaves, and so does an AI member whose opinion of the
founder falls below -25.

**Brussels.** Free movement opens Portugal's border, and so the Schengen area, to every member's
citizens. If Portugal adopts it while in the European Union, the Commission issues the Brussels
Ultimatum: a 365-day mission to suspend free movement or leave the Union. Suspend Free Movement (or
Remain in Europe in the European question) returns the community to its mobility agreement, and it
cannot adopt free movement again while Portugal stays in the Union. Choose the Community invokes
Article 50 without leaving NATO, and Atlantic Exit leaves both. Letting the mission run out costs
10% stability and 100 political power. Free movement then stays, but a federation still needs
Portugal out of the Union.

**The Iberian Union inherits it.** The union is a cosmetic name for the country that formed it,
so a Portuguese union keeps its community. The Ibero-American Community then opens it to the
Spanish-speaking world, and it becomes the Ibero-American Community. A Spanish union's
Lusophone Inheritance founds one with both languages open, or takes over the existing one if its
founder no longer exists. This is the friendly road to the Iberian Federation: the conquest
focuses of the founder branches remain the hostile one.

## The Iberian Union

The Iberian Union is no longer a formable decision. Spain and Portugal form it through a small
shared focus tree, The Dream of Iberia, which opens once the country owns and fully controls
mainland Spain, the Balearic Islands and mainland Portugal (Gibraltar and Andorra are not
needed). Portugal gets there through A Union of the Peninsula, which asks Madrid to join, or
by conquering Spain outright. The request takes 140 days, needs peace with Spain and more
than 25% influence there, and costs 400 political power.

Every region has a sectarianism score from 0 to 100, shown on a map in the Dream of an
Iberian Union decisions. Select a region on the map, then act on it:

- **Integration** (Iberian Civic Service, Centralised Schooling) raises its sectarianism now
  but lowers what it gains each week.
- **Concessions** (Regional Subsidies, Autonomy Statute) lower it now but raise what it
  gains each week.
- **Language classes** lower the union's sectarian growth, which starts at +0.25% a week, for
  an ongoing cost. Below 40% stability the union's growth cannot go below 0%.

Now and then an incident in one region, such as a fatal brawl or a teacher's viral video, raises
or lowers its sectarianism. A rise never strikes a region close enough to 100 to push it over.

The first focus already proclaims the country the Iberian Union and cores the union's
states; the rest of the tree is about holding it together. Sectarianism has two parts, the
score and its weekly growth, and each focus and decision shows the part it changes. The
tree builds a common curriculum, broadcasting, a single market and common citizenship, and
makes the capital choice: Lisbon or Madrid. Each capital calms its own side and angers the other.
Lisbon gains 16 victory points and Madrid loses 4, or Madrid gains 6 and Lisbon loses 1. Before
the proclamation the union also picks its language. Castilian or Portuguese costs 100 political
power, lowers sectarianism by 10 in its own country's states and raises it by 10 in the other's.
Two Official Languages adds 4 everywhere and a permanent spirit: 10% less political power, 5%
less stability and 10% higher bureaucracy costs. The Madrid-Lisbon High-Speed Line is optional. It opens once the
railway along the route is at level 6, and it calms the states it runs through and lowers the
union's sectarian growth. Once every region reaches 0, the union can be proclaimed: that ends
sectarianism and gives two years of National Unity (+10% stability, war support and political
power). A region that reaches 100 declares independence if it has a nation to break away as, and
the government either lets it go or goes to war to keep it. A region with no such nation falls
into unrest instead: the union takes the Regional Unrest spirit until its sectarianism drops
below 100.

After the proclamation, the Gates of Iberia lead outward (described above). Each founder also
gets a Balance of Powers branch that borrows from the other's own tree. A Portuguese union takes
**The Ibero-American Community** (influence and opinion across the Spanish-speaking world) and
works on Mexico, Cuba, Colombia and Venezuela. A Spanish union takes **The Lusophone Inheritance**
(the same across the Portuguese-speaking world) and works on Brazil, Angola, Mozambique and Timor
Leste. Each country has an opening focus that needs more than 10% influence there, costs 60 political
power, adds influence and trade opinion both ways, and takes the resource rights of a few
states whose owners are at peace with the union and under more than 20% of its influence: Mexican
Service Contracts (Mexico and Guatemala), the Antilles Partnership (Cuba and the Dominican Republic), the Andean Concessions
(Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia and Peru) and the Orinoco Belt (Venezuela), or the Brazilian
Partnership, the Angolan Oil Blocks, the Mozal Smelter and the Macau Forum. If the union founded
the community, the opening also invites its countries to join, with none of the invitation
decision's opinion and influence requirements. Each opening leads to a pressure focus that needs
75 political power, an intelligence network above 29% in the country, more than 20% influence
there and Regional Interventionism or higher. It costs the country 5% stability and war support
and gives the union 3% war support, and leads to a puppet war goal. A war goal needs a union
that is not Western Outlook, war support above 70%, Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism, and a target that exists and is neither in the
union's faction nor its subject.

**The Iberian Federation** ends each branch. In the Ibero-American branch it needs the four
opening focuses and 100 political power, and it invites each of Mexico, Cuba, Colombia and
Venezuela that is a subject or under more than 30% union influence and not at war with the union. A subject usually accepts. An independent country seldom does, though it is
likelier if it likes the union and shares its government. In the Lusophone branch it needs all four
chains complete and every one of the four countries that still exists a subject, and it invites
each subject. A country that accepts is annexed and its states become union cores. One that refuses
keeps its current status. The community above is the other friendly road: both founder branches
open it to the whole Ibero-American world.

## The Overseas Empire

Three Balance of Powers branches continue into the late game of an expansionist run. For a
walk through one, see the [Portugal expansion guide](/player-tutorials/portugal-expansion-guide/).

**The Southern Crown** follows the Braganza Restoration or the Sunrise Invasion. It opens once
the monarchy is restored and Portugal either has Brazil as a subject or owns Rio de Janeiro and
São Paulo. It annexes a Brazilian subject, cores every
Brazilian state Portugal owns and renames the country the United Kingdom of Portugal, Brazil
and the Algarves, or of Iberia, Brazil and the Algarves once the Iberian Union has formed. The court can move to Rio under a government that is not nationalist, unless
Portugal has started the Iberian union. The Platine Question claims Uruguay and leads to
war goals on Uruguay, Paraguay, Bolivia's Pando-Beni and then Argentina. Cayenne starts a border
war from Amapá into French Guiana. A border war is not a declared war, so NATO's mutual defence
does not trigger. The Uruguay, Paraguay, Pando-Beni and Cayenne focuses core the land they
target. If Portugal already owns that land, or already has Paraguay as a subject, the focus is
bypassed and still gives the cores. The Isthmus offers Panama a
security treaty that makes it an associated state. It needs more than 30% influence in Panama and
costs 60 political power. If Panama refuses, Portugal can take a war
goal instead. The United States' opinion of Portugal drops either way.

**The Estado da Índia** follows Full Sovereignty over Macau. It opens while Portugal still owns
Macau and is not democratic. It has war goals for
Goa, Damão and Diu, Malacca and then Singapore, Solor and Flores (with Timor held) and then the
Moluccas, and Hong Kong, which China defends. The Macau Window opens decisions that sell visas
to officials, passports to engineers and a tax haven to Chinese money, or broadcast to China.
Each one weakens China and adds a grievance. At every third grievance China retaliates with
six months of trade pressure, unless Portugal hands people back and clears the grievances.
With three grievances, A Second Opium War claims Guangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang and Shanghai, and
the Unequal Treaties raise compliance in the ports Portugal takes.

**The Crown of Aragon** follows the Gibraltar, Tangier or Andorra focus, so the union is already
proclaimed. Every focus in it that can lead to a war goal or a border war needs a union that is not
Western Outlook with war support above 70% and Global Interventionism or Neo-Imperialism. The Sardinian Question asks
Italy to free Sardinia. Italy almost always refuses, which gives the union a claim. The Sardinian
Protectorate then offers a free Sardinia protection as an associated state, or demands the island
from Italy, which can cede it or refuse and give the union a war goal. The Occitan Fiefs asks
France for Languedoc-Roussillon, Provence and Nice, the lands the Counts of Barcelona and the
Kings of Aragon held in the Middle Ages, and a refusal gives the union a war goal. With Nice held,
the Grimaldi Protectorate asks Monaco to become a puppet, as it was Spain's protectorate from
1524 to 1641. The Two Sicilies claims Naples, Sicily and
Calabria. The Knights' Isle after it asks Malta to become a puppet, and a refusal gives the union
a war goal. NATO only defends a member from an attacker outside the alliance, so a declared war on
Italy brings in the rest of NATO only once the union has left it. The focuses warn when that
applies. The Occitan Fiefs, Grimaldi Protectorate, Two Sicilies, Sardinian Protectorate and
Knights' Isle core the land they target. If the union already owns that land, or already has
the target as a subject, the focus is bypassed and still gives the cores. Mare Nostrum adds 3%
tax income, 10% naval coordination and 10% naval range to the Gates spirit. The Suez Question then claims the Suez state with a
war goal on whoever owns it, Egypt or a country that has taken it. If that owner capitulates,
the union takes Suez and makes the owner a puppet, puts its own party in power there with 15%
more influence, or leaves it independent.

**Territorial demands.** The focuses that claim another country's land, from Aljubarrota and
the Two Sicilies to Goa, Malacca, Hong Kong, Bahia and the Acre, send a demand first. The target
can cede the land, losing 10% stability and 5% war support, or refuse. A refusal gives Portugal
the claims and a war goal, and the target gains 2% stability and 5% war support. Its people
rally around the flag: the ruling party gains about 6% popularity, taken from every other party.
Refusing Tangier or the South Atlantic Islands brings the same rally. A country with a stronger
army than Portugal's always refuses unless it is Portugal's subject. The South Atlantic Islands,
above, is the exception.

**The League of Crowns.** Once the monarchy is restored, a Portuguese victory over Germany,
Austria, Greece, Romania, Bulgaria, Serbia, Hungary, Czechia, Belgium, the Netherlands, Denmark,
Norway or Sweden can end in a choice. The United Kingdom, France, Italy, Spain and Brazil get a
peace treaty instead, which has its own crown option. When
Portugal annexes one of them in the peace deal, it can keep the land and carry a three-year
stability, war support and political power penalty, release the country as a puppet, or release
it as an independent monarchy and found the League of Crowns. When Portugal puppets one, it can
keep the puppet or free it into the League. The League option needs Portugal to be in no
alliance, or to lead the League already. It gives Portugal an industrial pact for three years and
a League of Crowns bonus to stability, political power gain, war support and trade opinion that
grows with each member, up to eight. Later victories add new members to the same League.

## Peace Treaties

When a country in the table capitulates to Portugal, its subject, or a faction Portugal leads,
Portugal gets a treaty event the next day, after any peace conference. A union Spain founded gets
the same treaties once it has proclaimed the union, except those for Spain, Argentina, India,
China and Indonesia. A Spanish union that defeats Brazil gets the Overseas Charter, and its
treaty with the United Kingdom is Utrecht Undone. Each option draws the final border. The
states it names change hands, and anything else of the loser's that the victor took at the
conference goes back to it. A loser the conference annexed is released again first. The Canal
Treaty is different: it goes to whichever union completed the Suez Question when the owner of
Suez capitulates to anyone while at war with it. It only takes Suez and leaves the rest of the
conference result alone.

| Loser                                  | Treaty                 | Choices                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Spain                                  | Peace of Badajoz       | Aljubarrota claims, one crown, free nations, annex |
| Italy                                  | Peace of Naples        | Aragonese south, free Sardinia, crown in Rome      |
| France                                 | Peace of Perpignan     | Occitan fiefs, Corsica, Guiana; crown; Guiana      |
| UK                                     | Peace of Windsor       | Gibraltar and South Atlantic, end the Union, crown |
| Morocco                                | Treaty of Tangier      | Tangier, protectorate, free Sahara                 |
| Brazil                                 | Treaty of Rio          | North-east, Braganza crown, divide, leave jungle   |
| Argentina                              | Peace of the Plate     | Protectorate, or Mesopotamia and free Patagonia    |
| India                                  | Return to Goa          | Goa, Damão and Diu, or also Kerala and Mumbai      |
| China                                  | Pearl River Treaty     | Ports, or also free Tibet and East Turkestan       |
| Indonesia                              | Spice Islands Peace    | Spice route islands, West Timor to Timor           |
| Mexico, Cuba, Colombia, Venezuela      | Laws of the Indies     | Viceroyalty, friendly government, annex            |
| Angola, UNITA, Mozambique, Timor Leste | Overseas Charter       | Overseas province, friendly government, annex      |
| Paraguay                               | Peace of Asunción      | Protectorate, friendly government, annex           |
| Singapore                              | Straits Settlements    | Protectorate, friendly government, annex           |
| Monaco                                 | Grimaldi Protectorate  | Protectorate, friendly government, annex           |
| Malta                                  | The Knights' Isle      | Protectorate, friendly government, annex           |
| Sardinia                               | Sardinian Protectorate | Protectorate, friendly government, annex           |
| Panama                                 | Canal Zone             | The canal, the canal and a puppet, or annex        |
| Suez owner                             | Canal Treaty           | Suez plus puppet, friendly government or nothing   |

The crown options (one crown for Spain, a crown in Rome, a French or British crown, the Braganza
crown) need the restored monarchy. They make the loser a puppet, and outside Spain they put its
Monarchists in power with elections suspended.

The protectorate treaties end the puppet wars with three choices. A protectorate gives the
loser back its own states and makes it a puppet, and its neighbors' opinion of the victor falls
by 15. The viceroyalties and overseas provinces also take their colonial names, such as the
Viceroyalty of New Spain or the Overseas Province of Angola. A friendly government gives its
states back, puts the counterpart of the victor's ruling party in power with 15% more
popularity and adds 15% influence there. Annexing it costs 3% stability, and its neighbors'
opinion falls by 30. The Canal Zone always keeps the Panama state with the canal. Keeping only
that leaves Panama 40 opinion worse. The rest of Panama can instead become a puppet or be
annexed, at the same costs as above.

Leaving the jungle gives Brazil's Atlantic coast, from Maranhão to Rio Grande do Sul, to Vera
Cruz, a new Brazilian state that becomes Portugal's puppet, and leaves Brazil the interior and
the Amazon. If a player leads Brazil, they choose which of the two to play.

**Negotiated peace.** A war does not have to end in capitulation. Each month, if Portugal, or a
union Spain founded, fully controls every state an enemy owns that it claims or cores, or holds
its capital while the enemy is more than 30% of the way to surrender, it can offer terms: those states for peace, or
protection as a puppet. The enemy, or the player leading it, accepts or fights on. Either side
turning the offer down stops it for 180 days.

## Military Leadership

Portugal starts with the officers who held command on 1 January 2000: General Martins
Barrento at the head of the Army, General Espírito Santo as chief of the general staff,
Admiral Vieira Matias at the head of the Navy and General Alvarenga at the head of the Air
Force. The five starting brigades and military zones carry their 2000 commanders. Later chiefs
of staff join the pool on the date they took office.

## AI Behavior

The `POR_ai_behavior` game rule offers Historical, Lusophone, Imperial, Random Path and No
Path. Historical Portugal works the economy and the policy tree and leaves the networks
alone. Lusophone runs the cultivation and the puppeting. Imperial takes it to the Fifth
Empire.
