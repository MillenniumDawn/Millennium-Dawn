# U.S. Economic Foundations

The starting economy is unchanged. Tax legislation now changes real tax rates, service cuts have a cost, and domestic construction uses the existing paid effects. These are source calculations, not measured gameplay outcomes.

## Public construction and employment

Construction charges depend on projects actually eligible to run. Each explicit destination checks U.S. ownership and control. Paid effects retain their existing slot and location behavior. An office project includes the power plant supplied by the existing office effect.

| Project                             | Earlier charge |                                Revised charge | Employment or lasting effect               |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------: | --------------------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------ |
| Limited Industrial Expansion        |    $45 billion |                 $45 billion for six factories | Additional industrial capacity             |
| Early renewable investment          | $12.75 billion |         $25.5 billion for three installations | Existing renewable generation              |
| Federal Economic Funding Initiative |    $30 billion |          $48 billion for four office projects | Offices and their existing power provision |
| Public Works                        |    $60 billion | $10.5 billion for three infrastructure levels | Infrastructure; no direct employment grant |

[The complete focus ledger](foundations.csv) separates cash charges, recurring budget modifiers, worker requirements, and other permanent modifiers for every economic focus. Deltas are additive modifier values, not predicted percentage changes in GDP or unemployment. Conditional reward branches are listed as source totals and must not be treated as simultaneously executable outcomes.

Lower worker requirements free labor. They do not create positions. Service-related unemployment tolerance retains its existing meaning as social tolerance and is not counted as employment.

The dairy-aid restart and construction-supplier investment program each spend 0.2% of current GDP once. Dairy aid also raises the welfare-cost modifier by two points instead of reducing it. The ledger lists GDP-based costs and other treasury expressions separately from nominal fixed-dollar totals.
