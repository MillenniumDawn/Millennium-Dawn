Brazil starts in January 2000 as South America's largest economy, deep in debt and governed by Fernando Henrique Cardoso in his second and final term. It borders every South American country except Chile and Ecuador and starts with a population of 177.8 million.

## Ideology

The Brazilian Social Democracy Party (PSDB) rules in coalition with the Liberal Front Party and the Party of the Brazilian Democratic Movement. The Workers' Party (PT) is the main opposition, and the first election comes in October 2002.

The Brazil AI behavior game rule picks the path the AI follows: Historical, A New Brazil, The Red Republic, The Liberal Order, The Social Democrats, The Captain's Republic, or The Empire Restored.

## Diplomacy

Brazil is a member of Mercosur, the World Trade Organization, and the Antarctic Treaty. It starts with large commercial relations with Argentina, Chile, Mexico, and Germany, and close ties to the Portuguese-speaking countries of Africa.

Brazil's neighbors now give it more to do. Guyana, Suriname, and Trinidad and Tobago have resource decisions and historical events, from Guyana's Stabroek oil and Suriname's gold to Trinidad's gas and asphalt. Paraguay, Uruguay, Suriname, Guyana, and French Guiana have their real 2000 to 2025 parties and historical leaders.

## Military

Brazil starts with 25 divisions. The Army, Navy, and Air Force chiefs change on their historical dates through 2023.

## Factories

Brazil starts with 60 factories: 44 civilian industries, 13 military industries, and 3 naval dockyards. It also has 40 offices.

## Economy

Brazil starts with $48 billion in the treasury, $1,053 billion in debt (about half of GDP), and $124.8 billion in international investments. GDP is about $2.1 trillion, or $11.9 thousand per person.

Many economy focuses add to one spirit, State of the Brazilian Economy, instead of granting separate spirits. Three former spirits now raise it:

- **Effective Public Sector.** The focus, open only under José Serra, lowers bureaucracy costs by 10% and interest rates by 15%. It no longer speeds construction.
- **Push for Economic Liberalization.** It raises factory efficiency growth and cuts Foreign Investment costs by 10%.
- **Revitalize the Economy.** It raises productivity growth and research speed.

Brazil can sell Embraer aircraft to a friendly country through a Public Policy decision, for money and opinion. It allows one deal a year. Selling Embraer's commercial arm to Boeing raises the income from each sale.

## Unique National Features

- **Debt and the IMF.** The opening economy focuses ask the United States for debt relief. After Reevaluate Our Crippled Currency, Pay Back the IMF stays open while Brazil has any debt or currency spirit. It clears all of them and pays down part of the debt.
- **National Development.** One decision category holds Brazil's mines, ethanol, and great public works. Brazil starts with the first two excavation techs, and new mines appear as later ones are researched: Araxá and Catalão niobium, Trombetas and Juruti bauxite, Caetité and Santa Quitéria uranium, Autazes potash, and the Lithium Valley mines and refinery. The Energy and Mining Ministry focus adds the Carajás, Bahia chromite, and Serra Verde rare earth decisions. Major Development Projects adds the São Francisco transposition, the Tocantins basin, and the army, dockyard, and Amazon works.
- **Tailings dams.** Decommissioning the upstream dams in Minas Gerais costs steel output for two years but prevents the Mariana and Brumadinho collapses.
- **Ethanol.** After the sugarcane focus, Brazil can convert its fleet to flex-fuel, push ethanol exports, and run the fleet on ethanol during an oil shock.
- **The Amazon.** Conserved and wild acreage affect Stability, Political Power, research, and Non-Aligned support. Repeatable decisions fund rangers and reforestation or open the forest to logging and mining. Opening the forest costs more than protecting it, and each conservation decision makes foreign investment in Brazil cheaper and faster, up to 10%. From August 2008, with enough forest conserved, Brazil can ask Norway and Germany to fund the Amazon Fund.
- **Favelas and organized crime.** São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Bahia, Pernambuco, and Pará start with favelas that cost building slots and productivity growth. Favela decisions sit in the Cartels category.
  - **Cartel floor.** The gangs set a floor under the cartels. Cartel strength can't fall below 5 for each full favela and 2 for each upgraded one, and influence can't fall below 2 and 1. While any favela remains, both stay at 5 or more, so Brazil can't defeat the cartels. The favela decisions show the current floor.
  - **Clearing order.** Each favela must be pacified first, then rebuilt with housing, one project at a time. Clearing all five takes until about 2008 at the earliest.
  - **Narco concession.** If Brazil concedes to the narcos, every remaining favela becomes gang-ruled. Each gang-ruled favela adds 5 cartel strength and 3 influence to the narco state, which raises its bonuses and its drug money. The army can retake gang-ruled favelas one at a time through a Brazil-only decision. It costs 100 Political Power, about 1.5% of GDP, and stability, and takes a year.
  - **Dated events.** While the cartels survive, events cover the 2006 PCC attacks, the 2008 militia inquiry, the first pacification unit in Santa Marta in December 2008, the 2010 Alemão occupation, and the 2017 prison massacres.
- **Corruption.** Each scandal gives Brazil a choice: prosecute, which lowers corruption at a Jeitinho-scaled Political Power cost; strike a leniency deal that recovers money; take plea bargains that build evidence or expand Operation Car Wash; or cover it up for Political Power now, at the risk of a bigger scandal later. BNDES export credit can bring one scandal per borrower. Petrobras's first oil focuses bring the Contractors' Club, where looking away or funding the party starts the Petrolão scheme.
- **Operation Car Wash.** It opens only if Brazil took part in a scheme: through the Mensalão inquiry, or the March 2014 raids once a Petrolão scheme or plea-bargain evidence exists. A clean Brazil skips the investigation. Lula's arrest, release, and annulment decide whether the Workers' Party runs him or Fernando Haddad, and a three-way election follows.
- **Lula's choices.** As president, Lula decides on Palocci's primary surplus, the MST land occupations, the FTAA, the Growth Acceleration Program, the 2008 crisis response, and his succession. Each choice moves the Workers' Party balance between radicals and reformists.
- **National industry.** Brazil can found Terrabrás after self-funding the Goiás rare earths, refound ENGESA, fund Cobra Computadores, reactivate Telebrás from 2010, extend the Manaus Free Trade Zone incentives, and, on the socialist path, renationalize Vale. Dated events cover the 2000 Banespa sale and the 2006 Varig crisis.
- **Railways and chips.** The rail focuses rebuild the network through RFFSA, Valec, or concessions, from Norte-Sul to a Rio to São Paulo high-speed line. The CEITEC and PADIS focuses build microchip plants.
- **Cross-border projects.** Brazil can pay for roads, bridges, railways, and dams with its neighbors. Each is a decision that sends the partner an offer. If the partner accepts, the project opens months later with infrastructure on both sides, plus influence, opinion, trade, or resource exports. Each state a project improves needs room for another infrastructure level, and its controller needs the railway tech for that level. A state with no room when the project opens gets nothing.
  - Interoceanic Highway to Peru, from mid-2005.
  - Oyapock Bridge to French Guiana, offered to whoever owns it, from 2005.
  - Takutu River Bridge and a road and railway to Georgetown, from 2006. Guyana needs Post Conventional Railways.
  - Rail link to Uruguay at Rivera and Jaguarão, from 2006. Uruguay needs Post Conventional Railways.
  - Garabí and Panambi dams with Argentina, from 2008. Both countries need Basic Refining and a free slot for a renewable plant.
  - Twin-Ocean Railway to Lima, from May 2015, alone or with China once China completes its Five-Country Highway Corridor focus. Peru needs Post Conventional Railways.
  - Bioceanic Road Corridor from Porto Murtinho, from December 2015. Paraguay accepts first, then Argentina and Chile can join. The gains grow if the corridor reaches the Pacific.
- **Locked at the start.** Three projects wait for the partner's research. Guyana has only Integrated Transportation System, so the Takutu link waits for Post Conventional Railways. Uruguay and France have Post Conventional Railways, but Uruguay's northern state and French Guiana already have the infrastructure level that tech allows. The Uruguay rail link and the Oyapock Bridge wait until Uruguay and France research Early High Speed Rail.
- **Trade agreement offers.** From 2003 Brazil can offer Suriname, Chile, or an autonomous French Guiana a trade agreement, and the partner can decline. The Oyapock Bridge signs one with the owner of French Guiana when it opens. The Interoceanic Highway, the Takutu link, and the Uruguay rail link sign one with their partners.
- **Alcântara.** In August 2003 the VLS-1 rocket explodes on its pad. Brazil can rebuild and keep the VLS, which costs money and stability and gives a launch vehicle research bonus, or turn to foreign partners, which gives Political Power and makes the next offer cheaper. From late October 2003 Brazil can offer Ukraine Alcântara Cyclone Space, a joint company that flies Cyclone-4 rockets from the base. If Ukraine accepts, the launch complex opens about two years later with a research bonus, better launch reliability, and a trade agreement with Ukraine. In 2015 Brazil chooses to leave the company or keep paying for it. From December 2019 Brazil can offer the United States a Technology Safeguards Agreement that pays launch fees. If the United States accepts, the base's expansion threatens quilombola communities, and Brazil pays compensation or loses stability. The first three space focuses (Brazilian Space Program, Cooperate with NASA, and Create New Launchpads) now need regional power, and Create New Launchpads builds at Alcântara in Maranhão.
- **Kourou.** From December 2013 Brazil can ask whoever owns French Guiana to launch a satellite from Kourou. If the owner accepts, the launch costs Brazil $3 billion, or $2 billion once the Oyapock Bridge is open. About nine months later the satellite reaches orbit, with a research bonus for communications satellites, cheaper education, and better opinion. After France completes Open Kourou to Partners, Brazil can sign a launch-sharing deal with the owner of French Guiana, if the owner likes Brazil enough. Brazil pays $2 billion, each launch of its rockets then refunds 15% of each rocket's price, and the Kourou satellite launch costs $1 billion less. France's own focus gives it a 10% refund.
- **French Guiana.** When French Guiana votes on autonomy in January 2010, Brazil picks a side. Backing autonomy irritates Paris, and builds friendship and influence in Cayenne if French Guiana ends up autonomous. Siding with Paris improves French opinion. Staying out has no effect.
- **Operation Welcome.** In February 2018, while Venezuela's Chavista party governs and Brazil holds Roraima, Brazil can relocate Venezuelan families across the country, close the border, or leave it to Roraima. The Pacaraima clashes follow in August whatever Brazil picked. Brazil can send the Army, expand shelters and relocation, or leave it to the state government, which costs stability, and more if Brazil already left Roraima to cope.
- **The Essequibo crisis.** It starts with Venezuela's Decree 1787 in 2015 and runs through Venezuela's and Guyana's own events. If Venezuela proclaims a new state or mobilizes after its December 2023 referendum, Brazil can reinforce Roraima with a bunker, mediate between Caracas and Georgetown, or stay out. Mediating costs Political Power, less with the International Mediator spirit, and sends Brazil's envoy to the Argyle meeting for a Political Power reward and goodwill from Caribbean countries.
- **Venezuela's politics.** While the Chavista party governs, Brazil can recognize the disputed result of Venezuela's July 2024 election or withhold recognition until the tallies are published. At the Kazan summit in October 2024, a Brazil that belongs to BRICS can block Venezuela's bid to join or back it. The bid needs an India-led BRICS to exist.
- **Historical events.** Events run from the 2001 blackouts to COP30 in Belém in 2025, including the World Cup, the Olympics, the Rio Grande do Sul floods, and the 2025 US tariffs.
