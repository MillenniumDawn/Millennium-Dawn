Bosnia and Herzegovina begins in 2000 as a decentralized postwar state governed through the Dayton power-sharing system. The rotating presidency, international peacekeepers, landmines, weak institutions, and competing Bosniak, Croat, and Serb interests shape its early game.

## Overview

Bosnia's focus tree combines postwar reconstruction, constituent group management, military integration, and political realignment. A peaceful government can reconcile the entities, end foreign security oversight, and pursue Euro-Atlantic integration. Alternative paths can strengthen Republika Srpska, align with Russia and Serbia, restore Yugoslavia, empower organized labor, or abandon the Dayton settlement for an Islamist or military state.

The internal faction display tracks each constituent group's Attitude, Stability, and Manpower contribution. Poor management can produce demonstrations, secession crises, and renewed civil war. Economic branches address the 2008 financial crisis, landmine clearance, agriculture, mining, forestry, and regional reconstruction, while the military branch unifies the former entity forces and modernizes Bosnia's small army and air arm.
