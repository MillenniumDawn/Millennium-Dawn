# U.S. Financial Crisis Lifecycle

One systemic downturn follows the initial institutional responses. Regulation, Wall Street strength, energy exposure, and the completed responses determine its severity. Randomness selects duration within that severity tier.

## Historical sequence and prevention

Historical games schedule warnings in July 2007, Bear Stearns on March 16, 2008, Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac on September 7, Lehman on September 15, and the AIG response one day later. The institution sequence follows the [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/files/bernanke-lecture-three-20120327.pdf) and [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/history/2000-2009).

The three investigation stages each cost 25 political power once, replacing the old permanent political-power-income penalties. Closing an investigation grants 10 political power once rather than a permanent income bonus. Housing safeguards reduce historical crisis risk by 20. They continue to prevent nonhistorical onset. Calendar scheduling owns historical onset; nonhistorical warnings retain their existing policy-sensitive random dispatch.

## Initial responses and risk

Risk is calculated once when all four institutional responses have resolved:

`100 - regulation + Wall Street strength + energy exposure - intervention credit`

- The existing Iraqi energy crisis adds 10.
- Each of the three bank responses subtracts 10 for nationalization, 5 for a bailout, or zero for nonintervention.
- AIG subtracts 10 for an equity rescue, 5 for creditor support or a successful private consortium, or zero for nonintervention. Alternative flags are handled with exclusive branches.
- Completed historical housing safeguards subtract 20. Final risk is clamped to 0 through 150.

The ordinary economic cycle is lowered once, without improving an economy already in depression or charging the generic effect's repeated-downturn political-power penalty. One severity spirit owns the national emergency penalties.

## AIG, disruption, and recovery

Full Corporate History uses the detailed AIG chain. Other settings use a condensed initial response. Both use the same guarded payloads: equity or creditor support costs $40 billion once; successful private support and initial nonintervention cost no public money. Later containment or facility choices in the detailed chain are separate public commitments. The old parallel rescue and premature silent final-outcome marker are removed.

The downturn is queued only after all initial responses are known. Existing Canadian, Bosnian, and wider international hooks remain at the systemic downturn. Financial restructuring can continue after the emergency phase.

Recession incidents share a 90-day cooldown. Funded relief costs 0.2% of current GDP. Private adjustment gives a one-point stability penalty for 90 days through one shared spirit. Neither response destroys factories or adds another national output penalty.

The monthly pulse checks recovery directly after the severity spirit expires and the 180-day minimum has elapsed. Recovery is recorded when its notification is delivered, so an unanswered notification cannot queue duplicates. It clears any remaining local disruption but does not manufacture a generic economic boom.

Housing, financial, protest, and long-recession reports require an active crisis. Longer-term reports are scheduled relative to the downturn rather than unrelated calendar years. Their old overlapping national penalty ideas are removed.

AIG settlement spirits follow the same half-percentage-point structural reward ceiling. Interest adjustments use their actual additive percentage-point units, capped at 0.5 points; fractional national modifiers use 0.005. The outcomes remain mutually exclusive.
